Days When Fasting is Prohibited

In Islam, there are certain days when fasting is strictly prohibited. This prohibition is well-established and agreed upon by scholars.

Prohibited Days

Fasting is forbidden on the following five days:

  • Eid al-Fitr
  • Eid al-Adha
  • The Days of Tashreeq (11th, 12th, and 13th of Dhul-Hijjah)

The Prophet ﷺ forbade fasting on Eid al-Fitr and Eid al-Adha.

“The Messenger of Allah forbade fasting on two days: Eid al-Fitr and Eid al-Adha.”

(Reported by Muslim)

Days of Tashreeq

The days of Tashreeq are days of eating and drinking, therefore fasting on these days is not allowed.

“The days of Tashreeq are days of eating and drinking.”

(Reported by Muslim)

Day of Doubt (Yaum al-Shakk)

The Day of Doubt refers to the 30th of Sha'ban when it is unclear whether Ramadan has begun. Fasting on this day is prohibited, except for those who have a regular fasting habit such as Monday-Thursday fasting or the fast of Prophet Dawud.

“Whoever fasts on the day of doubt has disobeyed the Messenger of Allah.”

(Reported by Tirmidhi and others)

Important Note

The prohibition of fasting on these days shows that not all times are suitable for fasting. It is important for Muslims to understand which days are recommended and which are prohibited.

References: Muslim, Tirmidhi, and scholarly explanations.

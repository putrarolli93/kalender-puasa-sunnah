Fasting in Ramadan

Fasting during Ramadan is obligatory for every Muslim who is بالغ (mature), sane, healthy, and not traveling. This obligation is clearly stated in the Qur’an and agreed upon by all scholars. :contentReference[oaicite:0]{index=0}

Obligation

Allah commands fasting in Ramadan so that believers may attain piety.

“O you who believe, fasting has been prescribed for you as it was prescribed for those before you, so that you may become righteous.”

(Qur’an 2:183)

The Prophet ﷺ also confirmed that fasting in Ramadan is mandatory.

“The obligatory fast is the fast of Ramadan, unless you choose to do voluntary fasting.”

(Reported by Bukhari)

Warning

Intentionally breaking the fast without a valid excuse is a serious sin. There are strong warnings in hadith about those who break their fast before its proper time.

Conditions of Fasting

Fasting is valid when certain conditions are met, including being free from menstruation or postnatal bleeding and having the intention (niyyah).

Intention (Niyyah)

Intention is required for fasting, and it resides in the heart. It does not need to be spoken. A person who prepares for fasting, such as eating suhoor, already has intention.

“Actions are judged by intentions.”

(Reported by Bukhari and Muslim)

Timing of Intention

For Ramadan fasting, intention must be made before dawn each day. Each day of fasting is considered a separate act of worship.

“Whoever does not intend to fast before dawn, then there is no fast for him.”

(Reported by Abu Dawud, Tirmidhi, and An-Nasa’i)

Pillars of Fasting

The main pillar of fasting is to abstain from things that break the fast from dawn until sunset.

“Eat and drink until the white thread of dawn becomes distinct from the black thread, then complete the fast until night.”

(Qur’an 2:187)

Important Note

The time for fasting begins at true dawn (fajr) and ends at sunset. Eating shortly before dawn is allowed, and Islam encourages ease and does not intend hardship.

References: Qur’an (2:183, 2:187), Bukhari, Muslim, Abu Dawud, Tirmidhi, An-Nasa’i.
